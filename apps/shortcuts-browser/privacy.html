Privacy Policy

Shortcuts Browser

Overview

This policy explains how Shortcuts Browser ("the app") handles information when you use the iOS app. It distinguishes information stored locally by the app from information that may be collected by third-party services used to provide advertising.

Last updated: August 4, 2026

Information Stored by the App

Shortcuts Browser stores the following information in its app container on your device:

  • Browsing history: Website URLs opened through the app and the date and time they were opened.
  • Last opened URL: The most recent shortcut URL, used to coordinate shortcut launches.

This information is stored using iOS app-local preferences. The app does not operate a developer server that receives your browsing history or last opened URL.

Shortcut definitions are managed separately by Apple's Shortcuts app. Website content and website data are handled by Safari and the websites you visit under their own privacy practices.

The current app may include opened URLs in on-device diagnostic output for troubleshooting. Depending on your iOS diagnostic-sharing settings, system diagnostics may be handled by Apple. Avoid placing passwords, access tokens, or other secrets in shortcut URLs.

The categories below reproduce the declarations in the privacy manifest bundled with the SDK in the current app build. They do not mean that every category is collected from every person in every context; actual handling can vary by device, region, permission choices, and Google's configuration.

User Messaging Platform

The Google Mobile Ads package also contains Google's User Messaging Platform (UMP) component. Its bundled privacy manifest declares that it may collect the following data for app functionality. It declares these categories as not linked to you and not used for tracking.

  • Coarse location
  • Performance data
  • Product interaction

App versions that include the consent flow ask UMP for updated consent information when the app starts and, where required, display a Google consent form before requesting ads. When UMP indicates that privacy options must remain available, the app shows a privacy-choices control near the privacy-policy link. The forms and choices shown depend on your region and Google's configuration. Older installed versions may not include this control.

Tracking and Advertising Choices

Apple App Tracking Transparency

The app may ask for permission through Apple's App Tracking Transparency (ATT) prompt. If you allow tracking, the advertising SDK may access the advertising identifier and use information for tracking as described above. If you deny tracking, iOS restricts access to the advertising identifier and cross-app tracking. Denying ATT does not disable all ads or all data processing; contextual or non-personalized ads may still appear.

You can review or change this choice in Settings > Privacy & Security > Tracking on your device.

Google Advertising Choices

Where available, use the app's privacy-choices control to reopen Google's UMP options. You can also review Google's advertising controls in My Ad Center. Google's controls and availability vary by app version, account, region, and applicable law.

Retention and Deletion

  • Browsing history: Open the app's History screen and use the trash button to clear all history, or delete individual entries.
  • All app-managed local data: Delete the app from your device. This removes its app container, including browsing history and the last opened URL.
  • Shortcut definitions: Delete them separately in Apple's Shortcuts app.

Information processed by Google is retained and deleted under Google's own policies and controls. The app does not control Google's retention periods.
